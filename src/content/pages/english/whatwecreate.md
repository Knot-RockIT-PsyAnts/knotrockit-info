## Problem Statement & Social Need

In our modern society, digital services are facing increased criticism because they have significant impacts on social structures, privacy and economic development. The mass collection and analysis of user data not only enable precise user profiling but also comprehensive tracking of individual behavior.

This information is frequently used for targeted advertising or sold to third parties, often without the users’ explicit consent. Companies primarily pursue the goal of maximizing profits and design their services to capture as much time and attention as possible.

As a result, monopolistic structures emerge in which a few corporations control a large part of the digital market, claim sovereignty over the data, and thus possess economic and social power on an unprecedented scale.

These developments undermine democratic processes, exacerbate social inequalities and weaken people’s informational self-determination. Not least, this also leads to a massive erosion of the sense of community and international understanding. The problematic intertwining of commercial interests, opaque algorithms and a lack of regulation represents one of the central challenges in dealing with digital services.

![](/images/trenner2.webp)

Another significant problem here is that people are increasingly being pushed out of the focus of technological developments. Today, digital services are designed primarily with economic efficiency and profit maximization in mind, not with the well-being or self-determination of users.

Instead of aligning technologies with people’s actual needs, values and rights, the focus is often on data usability, click-through rates and dwell time. Algorithms optimize content for maximum attention and interaction - not for enlightenment, education, or social value. Users are reduced to mere data providers and targets of algorithmically driven advertising strategies.

The true purpose of technology - to serve people and improve their lives - is increasingly taking a back seat. This results in technological development that alienates people rather than empowering them.

## Solution approach

> Who's to say the Internet won't catch on someday after all?

It is extremely important to actively address these developments and strive for alternative, human-centered solutions. A society that accepts digital technologies without critical reflection risks losing fundamental values such as privacy, self-determination and social justice. To counteract this trend, we need to promote ethical technologies that are geared primarily toward the common good rather than profit.

We are addressing this issue because we are convinced that independent and reliable data is needed for the development of digital processes. We also need independent, data-efficient, and public-interest-oriented services and business models to ensure that European values - such as an understanding of democracy, data protection and privacy - can continue to be upheld in the future. Only when people are placed back at the center of technological development, digitalization can contribute to a fair, inclusive and sustainable future in the long term.

Addressing the problematic aspects of digital services is not only a technical and economic necessity but also a social and ethical one. The current structures of the digital world have a profound impact on democracy, opinions, education, human relationships and mental health.

For example, if a small number of corporations control the flow of information and manipulate people’s perception of reality through personalized content, the integrity of democratic societies is at stake. Trust in digital technologies is also increasingly eroded when people feel at the mercy of opaque systems whose inner workings they can neither understand nor influence.

![](/images/trenner2.webp)

Therefore, comprehensive public awareness and digital education are needed to limit the concentration of power and strengthen people’s digital rights. It must be made clear that technology is not used neutrally, but always serves specific interests - for example, those of the operators.

We see it as our responsibility to critically examine these interests and to reshape the relationship between technology and its providers. That is why we actively promote awareness regarding data and user protection and provide educational programs on the topic of the “digital future.” We are committed to encouraging science, tolerance, social participation and international understanding.

As a result of all these efforts, we envision new forms of digital coexistence: platforms that prioritize solidarity over competition, business models that promote sustainability rather than short-term profit, and digital spaces based on transparency, participation and respect for privacy. Only by promoting, utilizing and developing such alternatives, we can hope for a digital future that is not only efficient but also just, inclusive and truly humane.
