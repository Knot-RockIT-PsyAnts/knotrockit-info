##### What this label means

Privacy Permission Free is a label for applications that do not require any access to your personal data. No location, no contacts, no microphone, no camera, no files, no device identifiers. The application works completely without requiring you to grant a single permission.

The label is intended for you as a user who wants to take control of your own digital space. It helps you to instantly identify apps that respect your privacy from the very beginning.

![Why a label is necessary - placeholder image](/images/filler_14.webp)

##### Why a label is necessary

On smartphone app stores and in digital catalogues, you’ll find thousands of apps serving the same purpose. Their functions are often very similar. What makes them different remains hidden: what data is being transmitted in the background? What permissions does an app request that you may never have consciously noticed?

Privacy Permission Free makes this difference visible. The label gives you a clear criteria that you can use directly to make your decision. You don’t have to struggle through privacy policies or carry out technical analyses yourself. The label speaks for you.

![What “no access” actually means - placeholder image](/images/filler_11.webp)

##### What “no access” actually means

No access means: the app does not request any permissions, it is not granted any permissions, and it does not use any data in the background. This also applies to third-party libraries integrated into the app. No tracking service, no analytics platform, no cloud service that analyses your behaviour.

Specifically this excludes the following categories of data: location data via GPS, Wi-Fi or IP address; contacts and the address book; the camera and microphone; storage and files; biometric data such as fingerprints or facial recognition; device identifiers such as IMEI, MAC address or advertising ID; and sensor data that could be used to identify you.

There are no exceptions, even for seemingly harmless purposes such as statistics, error reports or an “enhanced user experience”. Any data request that goes beyond what is strictly functional contradicts the label.

![Why the label is binary - placeholder image](/images/filler_15.webp)

##### Why the label is binary

Privacy Permission Free accepts no levels of privacy. An application either fully meets the criteria or it does not carry the label. There is no such thing as “partially free” or “just a bit of tracking”.

The reason lies in technical reality: either an application can access your location or it can't. Either a service collects data about you or it doesn't. There is no grey area in between. A “little bit of access” is still access.

This clarity protects you from misunderstandings. You can trust the label because it leaves no room for interpretation.

![Privacy by Design - placeholder image](/images/filler_06.webp)

##### The basis: Privacy by Design

The label is based on data protection principles that describe how applications must be developed in order for this label to be granted.

Privacy by Design means that privacy is built into the architecture of an application right from the start. It is not an add-on that can be enabled via a setting; it is part of the fundamental design. An application developed according to this principle does not require any permissions because its design simply does not allow access to your data.

An application that has Privacy by Design embedded in its architecture does not need permissions. It cannot even request them without breaching its own fundamental principle.

![How we verify this - placeholder image](/images/filler_16.webp)

##### How we verify this

The label is not granted solely on the basis of self-declaration. We assess applications using automated analysis tools that examine the source code and the libraries incorporated into them. In addition, we check the application’s permission manifests and network activity.

Independent audits confirm the results of these checks at regular intervals. This ensures that the label remains valid even after an application has been updated.

##### What the label means to you

You make your decisions based on facts. You only share your data where you explicitly want to. You recognise applications that treat privacy not as an inconvenience, but as the foundation of their work.

Privacy Permission Free empowers your personal autonomy. You decide who knows what about you. The label is your tool for making this decision easily and with clarity.
