##### Clarity regarding the origin of content

TAIL - **T**ransparent **AI** **L**abel - is a labelling system that shows you at a glance how much artificial intelligence was involved in the creation of a piece of visual content. It is not about banning or demonising AI. It’s about creating transparency. As a viewer, you have the right to know whether an image was created entirely by a human, whether AI was used as a tool, or whether the content is predominantly machine-generated.

This label follows a tiered approach comprising five clearly defined levels. An image that is predominantly generated by AI differs fundamentally from one in which AI was used merely as an initial sketch of an idea. TAIL highlights this distinction and enables you to categorise content precisely according to its creation history.

![Placeholder image - Visible and machine-readable](/images/filler_26.webp)

##### Visible and machine-readable

TAIL anchors each level in two ways. On the content itself you find the symbol and its short description - recognisable at a glance, no tool required. We also store the level as a machine-readable tag in the file's metadata. A platform, browser extension or moderation system can read the label automatically.

Both channels carry the same value. What you see is exactly what a system reads.

![Placeholder image - Why five levels are necessary](/images/filler_22.webp)

##### Why five levels are necessary

Current case law and the public debate are increasingly calling for disclosure requirements for AI-generated content. However, a blanket label such as ‘AI-generated’ is not sufficient. It blurs the boundaries between human creativity and machine-generated output. A tool that an artist uses for inspiration must be assessed differently, both ethically and in terms of quality, from an algorithm that creates the majority of an image.

TAIL responds to these nuances by providing a detailed breakdown of the level of involvement. The system builds trust by avoiding generalisations. It specifies exactly what proportion of the final result is attributable to AI. This helps you avoid being misled and enables you to consume content in an informed manner. This transparency forms the basis for your decision to trust a piece of content or to scrutinise it critically.

##### The five levels of the TAIL label

The system divides AI involvement into five levels. Each level is indicated by its own symbol. The symbols are explained below:

<ImageText image="/images/tail_stage0_800.webp" alt="TAIL Symbol Level 0: No AI" title="Level 0: No AI">
This level denotes content that has been created entirely without the use of artificial intelligence. From the first sketch to the final pixel, the work is carried out exclusively by human hands. No AI tools were used for brainstorming, editing or content generation. The label here indicates: ‘Entirely human-created - without the use of AI’. This represents the highest degree of human authorship.
</ImageText>

<ImageText image="/images/tail_stage1_800.webp" alt="TAIL Symbol Level 1: AI as inspiration" title="Level 1: AI as inspiration">
In this category, AI served solely as a tool for generating ideas. It may have generated sketches, colour palettes or composition suggestions. However, the end result is entirely human-designed. Not a single pixel in the final image comes directly from the AI output. Humans have reworked, combined and recreated all the elements. The label reads: ‘AI as inspiration - entirely human-designed’. The AI provided the impetus, but was not a co-creator.
</ImageText>

<ImageText image="/images/tail_stage2_800.webp" alt="TAIL Symbol Level 2: AI as a partial contribution" title="Level 2: AI as a partial contribution">
Here, the AI has generated recognisable parts of the image or carried out significant preparatory work. A human has actively integrated these elements into a broader overall concept and edited them extensively. Around half, or less, of the visible result is based directly on AI-generated content, but has been corrected, supplemented and contextualised through human judgement. The label states: ‘Partially AI-generated - curated and supplemented by humans’. It signifies a genuine collaboration in which humans remain in control.
</ImageText>

<ImageText image="/images/tail_stage3_800.webp" alt="TAIL Symbol Level 3: AI as the main contributor" title="Level 3: AI as the main contributor">
At this level, the majority of the visual content is generated by AI. Human involvement is limited to targeted interventions, corrections or selecting from several AI-generated options. The basic structure, the colours and the main motifs are machine-generated. A human has reviewed and adjusted the result, but has not created it from scratch. The label clearly states: ‘Predominantly AI-generated - human-edited’. You can immediately tell that the machine did the bulk of the work.
</ImageText>

<ImageText image="/images/tail_stage4_800.webp" alt="TAIL Symbol Level 4: Entirely AI-generated" title="Level 4: Entirely AI-generated">
This level identifies content that has been created entirely by AI, without any human intervention in the creative process. We deliberately use such content only for non-critical areas such as abstract backgrounds, patterns or decorative graphics. We never use this level for depictions of people, brand logos or political content. The label reads: ‘Entirely AI-generated - without human design’. You know straight away that no human design process in the strict sense took place here.
</ImageText>

##### Legal and ethical underpinnings

The demand for transparency regarding AI-generated content is no longer a voluntary commitment, but is increasingly being enforced by law. Legislators and courts recognise that users must be protected from deception. The distinction between human and machine-generated content is becoming a matter of veracity in the digital sphere.

Transparency regarding the creation of content is required by law. Article 50 of the EU AI Act came into force on 2 August 2026 and obliges providers and users of AI systems to clearly label synthetically generated content. Deepfakes and texts of public interest must bear this label immediately; a transitional period until 2 December 2026 applies to the machine-readable labelling of images. TAIL is our response to this requirement with a tiered system. It breaks down the degree of AI involvement in detail and specifies exactly what proportion of a result is attributable to the machine. In this way, the legal requirement for disclosure becomes a reliable guide, enabling observers to make an informed assessment even before a general regulation covers every nuance.

##### How you benefit from the label

With TAIL, you don’t have to guess. You can see straight away where you stand. If pure human creativity is important to you, look for Level 0. If you recognise that AI serves as an efficient tool, you’ll accept Levels 1 or 2. If you want to avoid content that has been predominantly or entirely generated by machines, Levels 3 and 4 will help you make that distinction.

The label empowers you when engaging with digital media. It enables you to evaluate content not only by its impact, but also by how it was created. At a time when it’s hard to tell the real from the artificial, TAIL gives you back your compass. You decide what kind of content you want to see, share and trust.
